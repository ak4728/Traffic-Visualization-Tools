Ch. 4 §4.2 — stationary traffic states from CDOT detector stations — — drag the model parameters and watch R² ? Daganzo §4.2.1: if traffic is stationary, its states (q, k, v) lie on one curve — a property of the road, not of the traffic. k (or v) parameterizes the curve; q cannot, because every flow occurs at two speeds. The upper branch is unrestricted flow, the lower is queued (restricted) — caused from downstream.

Each dot is one real hour at a CDOT detector station (three stations, both directions, February and May 2025). Density is derived as k = q/u, with u the space-mean speed. Blue = unrestricted hours, red = queued (speed below 50 mph at density of at least 100 veh/mi) — note how they form the two branches of every diagram. Amber = slow at low density (speed below 50 mph with fewer than 100 veh/mi): snow and incident hours, mostly 12, 17, 20 and 21 February 2025. Those are not stationary states on the road's curve, so they are drawn but left out of every fit and R². Pick another station, direction or month in the selectors to see how the branches change.

Graphical readings (Fig. 4.4): on v–k, flow = area of the rectangle to the origin; on q–k, speed = slope of the ray from the origin (and the curve's own slope w is a wave speed); on v–q, 1/k = slope of the ray.

Greenshields (1935): linear u = uf(1 − k/kj) → parabolic q-k. Capacity = ufkj/4 at k = kj/2, i.e. at speed uf/2.
Underwood (1961): u = uf·e−k/kc — good in free flow, never reaches jam; capacity at speed uf/e ≈ 0.37 uf.
Northwestern (Drake, 1967): u = uf·e−½(k/kc)² — bell-shaped, a two-regime compromise; capacity at speed uf/√e ≈ 0.61 uf.
Pipes–Munjal (1967): u = uf·(1 − (k/kj)n) — Greenshields with an exponent; capacity at speed uf·n/(n+1), so n moves the knee (n = 1 is Greenshields, n = 3 puts it at 0.75 uf).
Newell (1961): u = uf·(1 − e−(λ/uf)(1/k − 1/kj)) — derived from car following; λ sets how sharp the knee is. It fits these stations best in the Python analysis.

Parameters that transfer. Every model here is driven by free-flow speed uf and capacity qmax, plus one shape slider for Pipes–Munjal (n) and Newell (λ); the jam or critical density is derived, so a fit from one station can be carried to another site where only uf and capacity are known. The two-parameter models fix "speed at capacity / uf" by their shape (½, 1/e, 1/√e) — compare it with the observed speed at the highest flows in the readout; the three-parameter models can move that knee, which is why they fit.

Drag the sliders to fit by eye — R² (of predicted vs observed speed at each density, over the blue and red hours) updates live. ⚡ auto-fit minimises, by default, the orthogonal distance from every hour to the curve in the normalised speed–flow plane (what the detector actually measures), holding uf within 0.85–1.2 × the measured free-flow speed; switch to the classic speed–density least squares to compare.

Reality check — why Greenshields is "of historical interest"
The parabola is forced to be symmetric: capacity must sit exactly at k₀/kj = 1/2. Daganzo notes real freeways have k₀/kj ≈ 1/6 — flow rises steeply to capacity, then falls off gradually. You can see it in the data on the q–k chart: on I-25 NB the apex sits near k ≈ 180–200 veh/mi, while the queued branch runs out past 400 veh/mi — a ratio near 1/2 only if you ignore the tail; on I-70 WB at Dahlia the apex is near k ≈ 135 veh/mi and the queued tail reaches 265 veh/mi. Drag uf and kj: you can match either the apex or the queued tail, never both. That tension is the whole argument for asymmetric shapes (and for the triangular diagram used in traffic dynamics, §4.3–4.4).

Which regime do you have data for? Switch to I-70 EB at Dahlia: that direction almost never leaves free flow (two months, maximum density under 200 veh/mi), so every model's jam density and capacity there is an extrapolation — Greenshields drifts to kj ≈ 545 and Underwood to a capacity above 12,000 veh/hr. Two miles east at Havana the same eastbound carriageway is the most congested series of all, with densities to 480 veh/mi. The models on the two-branch series (I-25 NB, I-70 WB at Dahlia, I-70 EB at Havana) agree within about 10% on capacity. A fundamental diagram is only as good as the congested data behind it.
