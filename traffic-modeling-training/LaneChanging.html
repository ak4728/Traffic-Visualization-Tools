Lane Changing — the MOBIL Model

Ch. 9 — Minimizing Overall Braking Induced by Lane changes (Treiber & Kesting Ch. 14) ? A two-lane road (wrapping around) with IDM cars and three slow trucks. Every second, each driver evaluates a change with two tests:

Safety criterion: the new follower must not be forced to brake harder than bsafe (Table 14.1: 2 m/s²).

MOBIL incentive (Eq. 14.7):
âc − ac + p·(Δafollowers) > Δa + abias
change only if my acceleration gain, plus the followers' gains/losses weighted by my politeness p, beats a threshold. p = 0: pure egoist (only safety protects others). p = 1: full altruist — changes only when they help traffic as a whole. p ≈ 0.2–0.4 matches real drivers.

The keep-right bias abias (0.3 m/s²) makes empty-road drivers drift right, modeling European rules; set 0 for US-style symmetric lanes.

Watch the readout: it prints the arithmetic of every executed change. Source: Kesting, Treiber & Helbing, "General lane-changing model MOBIL"; Treiber & Kesting, Traffic Flow Dynamics Ch. 14, traffic-simulation.de.

Two tests, in order 1) safety: âfollower ≥ −b_safe 2) incentive: âc − ac + p·(âf̂ − af̂ + âf − af) > Δa ± a_bias All accelerations are IDM evaluations of hypothetical situations: "how hard would I (and my old & new followers) accelerate if I were in the other lane right now?" The lane-changing brain reuses the car-following brain — that is MOBIL's core idea.
Experiments
1) p = 0, Δa = 0: hyperactive egoists — watch the changes-per-minute counter and the left lane clog. 2) p = 1: near-silence; only clearly beneficial overtakes happen. 3) bias = 0: lanes become symmetric and trucks wander left (US style). 4) Raise b_safe to 4: aggressive gap acceptance — safety refusals drop, but new followers brake visibly hard.
