Car-Following Models

Ch. 8 — the city start-stop scenario from Treiber & Kesting Ch. 10–11: same road, four brains ? Fifteen vehicles queue at a red light, accelerate on green, cruise, then hit a second red — the "city scenario" the book uses as a fact sheet for every model (§10.5). Pick a model and watch the same situation handled by four different acceleration laws:

OVM (Bando, Eq. 10.19): relax toward an optimal speed vopt(gap) with time constant τ. Famous flaw: no reaction to closing speed — τ = 0.65 s is needed to avoid crashes but produces absurd ±20 m/s² accelerations; raise τ a little and vehicles collide (§10.6).

FVDM (Eq. 10.23): adds a speed-difference term −γΔv. Accident-free with a relaxed τ — but the Δv term ignores distance, so a red light far ahead slows cars that should still be driving (the book's "incomplete model" lesson).

Gipps (Eq. 11.10–11.11): drive at min(v + aΔt, v₀, vsafe) where vsafe guarantees stopping behind the leader even in the worst case. Simplest complete accident-free model.

IDM (Eq. 11.3): smooth acceleration a[1 − (v/v₀)⁴ − (s*/s)²] with dynamic desired gap s* — the modern standard (and the engine of this site's games).

Source: M. Treiber & A. Kesting, Traffic Flow Dynamics (Springer 2013), traffic-simulation.de.
