Dynamic Macroscopic Models — LWR

Ch. 4 §4.4 — Lighthill-Whitham-Richards: conservation + a fundamental diagram = a full theory of traffic dynamics ? The LWR model needs only two ingredients:
1. the conservation law ∂q/∂x + ∂k/∂t = 0 (§4.3), and
2. the assumption that traffic is always on the fundamental diagram: q = Q(k) (§4.2 — Greenshields here).

Substituting 2 into 1 gives ∂k/∂t + Q′(k)·∂k/∂x = 0: density is carried along waves (characteristics) — straight lines of slope w = Q′(k) = v_f(1 − 2k/k_j), the tangent to the FD. Waves are slower than cars (w ≤ v): drivers respond to what happens ahead.

When faster waves run into slower ones they collide and form a shock whose speed is the chord slope U = Δq/Δk (Eq. 4.34). When waves spread apart, an expansion fan fills the gap.

The t-x field is colored by density (light = empty, dark red = jam), thin lines are waves, the bold dark red line is the shock, gray curves are vehicle trajectories (always crossing waves forward).

Why waves ≠ vehicles
A wave carries a traffic condition (a density value), not a car. Its slope is the FD's tangent Q′(k); a car's slope is the ray v = q/k — always steeper. So trajectories cross waves from behind: news travels backward through traffic. In the signal scenario the queue's tail grows at the chord speed U₁ < 0 while the release wave from the green eats it back — where the two shocks meet, the queue dies.
Try this
1) Shock: raise k_d toward jam — the chord tilts down and the shock races backward. 2) Fan: the fan's rays are the FD tangents between the two states; vehicles thread smoothly through it. 3) Signal: lengthen the red — the maximum queue and the time to clear both grow; the discharge wave always releases flow at capacity (k = k_j/2) right at the stop line, which is why saturation flow is a property of the road.
