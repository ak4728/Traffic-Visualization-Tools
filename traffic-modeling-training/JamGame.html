🎮 Dissolve the Phantom Jam

Ch. 4 game — stop-and-go waves on a ring road (IDM car-following) ? Cars drive around a 600 m ring following the Intelligent Driver Model (Treiber): each car accelerates toward its desired speed but keeps a safe dynamic gap s* = s₀ + vT + vΔv/(2√(ab)) to its leader. Above a critical density the equilibrium is unstable: one tap of the brakes grows into a backward-travelling stop-and-go wave — a jam with no cause, the phenomenon behind Chapter 4's congested branch.

Your job: hit ⚡ to trigger the jam, then dissolve it using the speed limit. A well-chosen temporary limit stops cars from racing into the back of the queue faster than it discharges, starving the wave until it evaporates — the principle behind variable speed limits on managed motorways. Then release the limit and see whether the jam returns.

Score = average ring speed over the last 30 s. Beat 80% of free-flow with 28 cars on the ring and you have earned your variable-speed-limit badge.

Inspired by traffic-simulation.de (M. Treiber & A. Kesting, whose textbook covers the IDM in depth).

Why the jam is "phantom"
Every driver reacts a little late and a little too hard (finite T and b in the IDM). Above the critical density those over-reactions amplify from car to car instead of dying out — the string is unstable. The wave itself rolls backward at roughly the congested-branch wave speed from Chapter 4 while every car moves forward through it.
Playbook
1) 28 cars, ⚡, watch the wave form and roll backward. 2) Set the limit to ~18–24 mph: arrivals at the queue tail slow to the discharge rate and the wave starves. 3) Release the limit gradually. 4) Try 35+ cars — above a certain density no limit saves you: the equilibrium itself is queued. That density is the ring's version of capacity.
