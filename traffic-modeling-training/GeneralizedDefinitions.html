Generalized Definitions of Flow, Density & Speed

Ch. 4 §4.1.1 — Daganzo / Edie (1963): measure any region A of the time-space plane ? A time-space diagram with real (simulated) trajectories through a temporary bottleneck. Drag region A anywhere, resize it by its corner handles, or shear it with the observer-speed slider.

Edie's generalized definitions (Daganzo Eqs. 4.3, 4.8, 4.12):
k(A) = t(A) / |A| — total vehicle-time in A ÷ 'area' of A
q(A) = d(A) / |A| — total distance driven in A ÷ 'area' of A
v(A) = q(A) / k(A) = d(A) / t(A) — a true space-mean speed

|A| has units of distance × time. The identity q = k·v is exact for these definitions — no matter what region you pick, unlike mixing a detector's flow with a photo's density.

Presets: a photograph (thin vertical strip → k = n/L, v = arithmetic mean of photo speeds, Eq. 4.14); a detector (thin horizontal strip → q = m/T, v = harmonic mean of passing speeds, Eqs. 4.15–4.16 — this is why space-mean ≤ time-mean); a generic L × T rectangle; and a moving observer (sheared parallelogram travelling at speed w).

Why this matters
A detector measures over time at a point; a photo measures over space at an instant. Mixing the two ("detector q" ÷ "photo k") breaks q = k·v. Edie's region-based definitions always satisfy it — and they let you compare streams observed in completely different ways. Note the point limit is ill-defined: shrink A onto a spot between trajectories and k → 0; shrink it onto a trajectory and k → ∞ (Daganzo's points O and P). Density is a property of a region, not a point.
Try this
1) Set the Detector preset and drag it into the queue — v(A) is the harmonic mean of passing speeds (Eq. 4.16), which is why it dips below the arithmetic mean. 2) Set Photograph and slide it across the shockwave — watch k jump as the strip crosses into the queue. 3) Pick Moving observer and set w to the wave speed (≈ −6 mph): travelling with the shockwave, conditions inside A stay nearly constant.
