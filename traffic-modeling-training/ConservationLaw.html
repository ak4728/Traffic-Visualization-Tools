The Conservation Law

Ch. 4 §4.3 — vehicles are neither lost nor created: count them in, count them out ? Same simulated traffic as the Generalized Definitions page (a temporary bottleneck builds a queue and a backward shockwave).

Counting box (Daganzo Fig. 4.8): drag/resize the box. Vehicles enter through the bottom side (m₁ — crossing the upstream location) or the left side (n₁ — already inside at the start), and leave through the top (m₂) or the right (n₂ — still inside at the end). Conservation says m₁+n₁ = m₂+n₂ for any box — shrink it and (m₂−m₁)+(n₂−n₁)=0 becomes ∂q/∂x + ∂k/∂t = 0 (Eq. 4.28).

Moving observer (Eq. 4.32): a traveller at speed v° is passed by q°·T = (q − k·v°)·T vehicles net. We count the actual crossings of her trajectory and compare with the formula, using Edie's q, k measured in a thin band along her path.

Interface velocity (Eq. 4.34): between two stationary states, conservation alone forces the interface to move at U = Δq/Δk — the chord slope between the two states on the q–k diagram. We compare that with the shockwave the simulation actually produced.

Shrink the box to dt × dx: with m ≈ q·dt and n ≈ k·dx, the identity becomes ∂q/∂x + ∂k/∂t = 0 — the conservation equation. Its very existence is equivalent to being able to draw an N(t, x) surface: one label per trajectory, no source, no sink. If a vehicle were born inside the box (an on-ramp), no consistent corner labels would exist — that is why ramps force the extended form ∂q/∂x + ∂k/∂t = g(t, x).
