iThe queueing (input–output) diagram: the blue curve is cumulative demand A(t); the green curve is what the bottleneck of capacity μ can discharge, D(t). While demand exceeds capacity a queue grows — the vertical gap is the queue, the horizontal gap at height n is vehicle n's delay, and the shaded area between the curves is total delay (veh·min). Push the peak demand above capacity and watch the triangle of delay grow; the queue keeps growing until demand falls back below μ, and clears where the curves rejoin.

iDeterministic signal queueing (D/D/1): vehicles arrive at steady rate λ; the stop line serves 0 during red and at saturation flow s during green. Each cycle the departure staircase falls behind by λ·r vehicles and catches up at rate s once green starts. The shaded triangles are delay per cycle; average (uniform) delay per vehicle is d = r² / (2C(1−λ/s)). If λ·C > s·g the green can't clear what a cycle brings — oversaturation: a residual queue carries over and grows every cycle.
