Ch. 6 — how long must you count, and why correlated samples lie ? Counting error (§6.1.4, §6.2.1): vehicle arrivals behave like a Poisson process, so a count N in a window T has variance equal to its mean. The flow estimate q̂ = N/T then has relative error 1/√(counted vehicles) — to halve the error you must count four times as long. The histogram shows 400 simulated observation windows; the smooth curve is the normal approximation.

Headways of a Poisson process are exponential ("forgetful", §6.1.5): the histogram in the corner shows the simulated headways against the exponential density.

Correlated samples (§6.2.1.1, §6.2.2): a queue evolves slowly — successive cycles remember each other. Averaging one contiguous stretch of W cycles is NOT worth W independent observations: its error stops shrinking once W is short compared with the queue's relaxation time. The chart compares the error of contiguous-window averages against genuinely independent samples of the same size.
