🎮 Ramp Metering

Ch. 5 game — protect the mainline without strangling the ramp ? A 1.2 km freeway (IDM cars) with a busy on-ramp. Every merging car disturbs the mainline; too many merges too fast and the disturbances grow into a breakdown that rolls upstream — then everybody loses. Too slow a meter and the ramp queue explodes instead.

Your control: the metering rate (one car per green). Total delay = mainline slowdown + ramp waiting, in vehicle-minutes, accumulated over a 3-minute episode.

Goal: finish an episode with less total delay than the "no meter" baseline — then try to beat your own best. The sweet spot releases just under the spare mainline capacity at the merge, which is exactly the logic of ALINEA-style feedback metering.

Inspired by the ramp-metering game at traffic-simulation.de (M. Treiber & A. Kesting).

The tradeoff you are steering
The merge is a bottleneck: mainline demand + ramp releases must stay below its capacity or the freeway breaks down and discharges below capacity (the capacity-drop). Metering rations the ramp so the sum stays just under the line — mainline delay is bought down with ramp delay. The optimum is not "meter hard": every vehicle held on the ramp is delay too.
Playbook
1) Run once with the meter wide open (24/min ≈ no meter) — watch the breakdown roll upstream. 2) Run at 6/min — mainline flows but the ramp queue balloons. 3) Find the rate that minimizes the total. 4) Raise mainline demand to 2,000 and find it again — the optimal rate falls as spare capacity shrinks.
