🎮 Ramp Metering

Ch. 5 game — a full micro-simulation: IDM car-following + MOBIL lane changing + gap-accepted merging ? A 1.2 km two-lane freeway (IDM cars) with a busy on-ramp joining the right lane. Mainline drivers run MOBIL lane changes: they overtake slow traffic, drift back right (keep-right bias), and — near the merge — shift left to open the right lane for entering vehicles, the "zipper courtesy" you see on real freeways. Ramp cars queue at the meter, launch on green, and yield at the nose until an IDM-checked gap in the right lane actually works.

Your control: the metering rate (one car per green). Total delay = mainline slowdown + ramp waiting, in vehicle-minutes, over a 3-minute episode.

Goal: minimize total delay. Metering hard protects the mainline but drowns the ramp; wide open floods the right lane and the disturbance spreads to both lanes through lane changing. The sweet spot is ALINEA's logic: release just under the spare capacity of the merge.

Models: IDM & MOBIL from Treiber & Kesting, Traffic Flow Dynamics (Ch. 11, 14); game inspired by traffic-simulation.de.

Three models, one road
IDM keeps every car a safe dynamic gap behind its leader. MOBIL decides lane changes: safety first (new follower braking ≤ b_safe), then incentive with politeness p = 0.3 and a keep-right bias that flips to keep-left in the 250 m before the merge — so courtesy for merging traffic emerges from the model, not from a script. Gap acceptance governs the nose: a launched ramp car enters only when neither it nor the right-lane follower would need an impossible deceleration; otherwise it yields.
Playbook
1) Meter wide open (20/min): watch the right lane sag at the merge and the disturbance leak into the left lane via lane changes. 2) 3/min: the mainline glides but the ramp queue explodes. 3) Find the minimum-total rate. 4) Push mainline demand to 2,400 — the optimum drops as spare capacity shrinks, and the courtesy shift to the left lane becomes easy to spot.
